Date: 2026-05-26

Policy version: `v1.0`

Status: research baseline only. This is not a buy or sell instruction.

## Starting Premise

The satellite account should search for companies positioned at structural
bottlenecks where long-term compounding can be nonlinear:

- space launch, space systems, direct-to-device communications, and defense
  space infrastructure;
- AI data-center interconnect, power, cooling, and cloud capacity;
- quantum computing and quantum networking;
- stablecoin and programmable money infrastructure;
- future public companies that become directly tradable under policy.

The user's initial list is useful but not authoritative. Every company must
earn its place through fresh evidence.

## Current Candidate View

RKLB: A

Rocket Lab currently looks like the strongest execution-led public space
platform in the initial universe. Q1 2026 reported revenue of USD 200.3 million,
up 63.5% year over year, with record GAAP gross margin of 38.2% and backlog of
USD 2.2 billion. The long-term upside depends on whether Rocket Lab can compound
from Electron and space systems into a broader launch and space infrastructure
platform. Neutron remains the key upside and execution-risk variable.

Kill or downgrade signals: major Neutron slippage without credible mitigation,
backlog failing to convert into revenue, space systems margin deterioration,
heavy dilution without matching strategic progress, or loss of launch
reliability.

ASTS: A

AST SpaceMobile has one of the highest-upside theses because direct-to-device
satellite broadband could become a new global connectivity layer. Q1 2026
evidence included FCC authorization for commercial SpaceMobile service in the
United States, a target of about 45 satellites in orbit during 2026, 2026
revenue guidance of USD 150 million to USD 200 million, and about USD 3.5
billion of cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash at quarter end. This is
still execution-heavy and binary relative to more mature companies.

Kill or downgrade signals: repeated launch or satellite production delays,
regulatory setbacks, inability to convert mobile network operator partnerships
into material revenue, uncontrolled cash burn, or dilution that damages
long-term per-share economics.

CRDO: A-

Credo is a strong AI infrastructure bottleneck candidate through high-speed,
energy-efficient connectivity. Fiscal Q3 2026 revenue was USD 407.0 million,
up more than 50% sequentially and about 200% year over year, with strong margins
and a large cash and short-term investment balance. The thesis is attractive,
but customer concentration, AI capex cyclicality, and valuation can quickly
matter.

Kill or downgrade signals: loss or slowing of key hyperscaler customers,
margin compression from competition, failure to diversify product ramps, or a
technology route that bypasses Credo's products.

ALAB: A-

Astera Labs is another AI rack-scale connectivity candidate. Q1 2026 revenue
was USD 308.4 million, up 14% sequentially and 93% year over year, with GAAP
gross margin of 76.3%. The company has high-quality exposure to PCIe, CXL,
Ethernet, NVLink Fusion, UALink, and scale-up or scale-out AI fabrics. The
primary risk is that market expectations and customer concentration leave little
room for execution mistakes.

Kill or downgrade signals: AI infrastructure order slowdown, inventory
correction, customer concentration worsening, design-win losses, or gross margin
compression that suggests commoditization.

VRT: A-

Vertiv is more mature than the highest-upside small-cap names, but it sits on a
real AI data-center physical infrastructure bottleneck: power, cooling, thermal
management, and services. Q1 2026 net sales were USD 2.65 billion, up 30% from
Q1 2025, with raised full-year 2026 net sales guidance of USD 13.5 billion to
USD 14.0 billion and adjusted free cash flow guidance of USD 2.1 billion to
USD 2.3 billion. The likely upside is less explosive than ASTS, RKLB, or early
AI hardware names, but the evidence base is strong.

Kill or downgrade signals: AI data-center orders cancelled or deferred, margin
pressure from supply constraints, liquid-cooling execution failure, or backlog
quality weakening.

NBIS: B+

Nebius is an AI cloud and capacity candidate. It can benefit if GPU capacity,
power access, and AI factory execution remain scarce. The risk profile is high:
large capex, financing needs, customer concentration, receivables quality, and
possible circular AI infrastructure financing. It belongs on the watchlist, but
requires fresh 6-K/20-F and contract-level checks before any allocation.

Kill or downgrade signals: receivables growth far outpacing revenue quality,
financing dilution, customer concentration worsening, power or GPU delivery
delays, or poor utilization economics.

CRCL: B+

Circle is a financial infrastructure candidate through USDC, reserve income,
payments, and programmable money. Q1 2026 total revenue and reserve income were
reported at USD 694 million, and USDC circulation was reported around USD 77
billion. The long-term upside depends on whether Circle can become a durable
toll point for regulated internet dollars rather than a rate-sensitive reserve
income business.

Kill or downgrade signals: USDC market-share loss, distribution costs consuming
economics, rate declines without offsetting transaction revenue, adverse
stablecoin regulation, or banks and exchanges capturing the economics.

CBRS: B

Cerebras is a newly public AI infrastructure company. The IPO closed on
2026-05-15, and the Class A shares began trading on Nasdaq under `CBRS` on
2026-05-14. The technology and market ambition are significant, but the public
track record is too short for core status. It should normally wait for at least
two public quarterly reports unless fresh primary evidence becomes unusually
strong.

Kill or downgrade signals: customer concentration not improving, backlog not
turning into revenue, gross margin quality weaker than expected, supply chain
constraints, or valuation already discounting too much success.

BE: B

Bloom Energy may benefit from AI data-center power shortages and onsite power
demand. Q1 2026 revenue was USD 751.1 million, up 130.4% year over year, and
the company raised full-year 2026 guidance. The opportunity is real, but the
history of fuel-cell economics, service obligations, customer concentration, and
order quality require skeptical tracking.

Kill or downgrade signals: service costs rising, customer economics weakening,
orders relying on subsidies or one-off structures, cash flow failing to follow
revenue, or warranty issues.

IONQ: B-

IonQ is an option-like quantum candidate. Q1 2026 guidance was raised to
USD 260 million to USD 270 million of full-year revenue, while adjusted EBITDA
loss guidance remained deeply negative. The upside could be enormous if quantum
computing, networking, sensing, or security becomes commercially essential, but
the current evidence is still early.

Kill or downgrade signals: revenue driven mostly by one-time system sales,
commercial demand failing to broaden, burn rate rising without technical
milestones, or credible competing architectures taking the lead.

LUNR: B-

Intuitive Machines has improved evidence through lunar, civil, commercial, and
national security space infrastructure. Q1 2026 revenue was USD 186.7 million,
backlog was USD 1.1 billion, and full-year 2026 revenue outlook was USD 900
million to USD 1.0 billion. The risk remains high because mission execution,
government budget timing, acquisitions, and integration can dominate results.

Kill or downgrade signals: mission failures, acquisition integration problems,
backlog margin weakness, budget delays, or cash burn not improving.

RDW: C+

Redwire fits the space infrastructure and defense theme, but the company is more
complex. It needs more evidence on integration, internal controls, margin, cash
flow, and customer quality before being treated as a serious initial allocation
candidate.

Kill or downgrade signals: integration failures, internal-control issues,
weak cash generation, customer concentration, or financing pressure.

## Baseline Conclusion

The first active research pool should focus on:

- core candidates: RKLB, ASTS;
- near-core AI infrastructure candidates: CRDO, ALAB, VRT;
- watch candidates: NBIS, CRCL, CBRS, BE, IONQ, LUNR;
- research-only candidate: RDW;
- future IPO watches: SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic.

This does not mean the first real monthly deposit should buy all core
candidates. The first real decision must depend on confirmed cash, live prices,
fresh facts, current valuation, and whether whole or fractional shares are
available.
