## Zero Versus Mission-Consistent Exposure

Twenty-nine shares at the USD 9.60 limit would cost at most USD 278.40, or 2.0413 percent of research NAV. Zero is inferior because financing covers the remaining base consideration without an evident liquidity cliff, the acquired segment already has positive operating economics, and the current valuation leaves a credible evidence-gated path to scale. Twenty-eight shares would fall below the recorded two-percent floor, while every quantity above 29 would assume combined cash-conversion and integration evidence that does not yet exist.

Analytical stress prices of USD 4.80, USD 14, USD 28 and USD 60 are not targets. At maximum starter cost, they imply approximate NAV effects of -1.0207, +0.9356, +3.9125 and +10.7160 percent. The exceptional case requires a durable multi-orbit ground and defense platform rather than merely a successful closing.

## Bull, Bear and Net Weight

The bull case is direct ground and defense exposure, profitable standalone operations, positive acquired-segment operating economics, a material current order, funded closing capacity and a lower starting denominator. The bear case is that closing adjustments, working capital, integration, negative cash conversion, customer loss or dilution absorb the apparent value before it reaches common shareholders.

The bull case now controls only at the minimum mission-consistent weight. Size, price and expiry bound the remaining uncertainty; they do not justify assuming it away. The recommendation is therefore exactly 29 shares at no more than USD 9.60 for the September 11 regular session, with every larger quantity zero.
