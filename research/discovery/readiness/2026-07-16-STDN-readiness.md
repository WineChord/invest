## Conclusion

Standard Nuclear is a material, direct advanced-nuclear fuel-cycle lead, not a keyword false positive. The reachable public research is complete, but the security had no final Form 424B4 or completed regular-session close at the decision cutoff. The expected USD 15 offering price is an underwriting term, not a market-data substitute. The correct decision is `not_tradable`, buy 0, and rerun entry analysis after the first completed close.

## Bottleneck and mission fit

TRISO, or tristructural-isotropic particle fuel, encapsulates uranium fuel in multiple ceramic and carbon layers. It matters because several advanced-reactor designs require a qualified, scalable supply of this specialized fuel. Standard Nuclear's manufacturing focus is therefore more direct to the fuel bottleneck than a reactor developer, and it improves comparison with LEU, NNE, NUCL, OKLO, SMR, and IMSR.

## Primary evidence

The July 15, 2026 S-1/A expected a 10.0 million-share IPO at USD 15 and a 1.5 million-share option. After the base offering, the filing showed 149,095,234 Class A and 11,578,308 Class B shares, or 160,673,542 basic shares, implying approximately USD 2.410 billion at the expected price. Outstanding options and planned IPO grants raise the granted-diluted reference value to at least approximately USD 2.735 billion.

Trailing revenue was approximately USD 3.356 million using FY2025 plus Q1 2026 less Q1 2025. The basic expected-price reference is therefore roughly 718 times trailing sales. The business remains pre-scale: 2025 cost of goods sold exceeded revenue, and Q1 2026 cost of goods sold also exceeded revenue. The filing disclosed USD 65 million of funded backlog, USD 157 million of unexercised customer purchase options, and USD 23 million of contingent unfunded backlog; only the funded USD 65 million is decision-grade demand. A June 2026 first commercial delivery of more than 100 pounds, or about 50 kilograms uranium, is real operating evidence, but current capacity and planned expansion remain far below long-term targets.

Preliminary June 30 cash was USD 95–105 million, and expected base IPO net proceeds were approximately USD 136.5 million. Near-term survival therefore appears adequate, but commissioning, scale-up, customer concentration, advanced-reactor schedules, uranium feedstock, negative gross economics, dual-class control, option grants, and future dilution remain material.

## Decision gates

- Mission: pass. Direct scarce-fuel exposure could create multi-decade bottleneck value.
- Evidence: fail for a buy. Commercial evidence is early and non-funded options cannot be treated as backlog.
- Entry: fail. The expected-price reference implies extreme sales multiples, and no completed market price exists.
- Survival: near-term pass, long-term uncertain. IPO liquidity helps, but manufacturing scale and margin proof are absent.
- Opportunity cost: LEU has materially stronger operating and public evidence; existing space and nuclear risks already compete for cash.

## Triggers and invalidation

Reject the thesis if commercial qualification, customer programs, supply, or manufacturing economics fail to scale before capital needs overwhelm per-share value.
