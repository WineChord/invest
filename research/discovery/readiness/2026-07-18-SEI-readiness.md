## Conclusion

Solaris Energy Infrastructure is a direct, operating expression of rapidly deployable behind-the-meter power for artificial-intelligence data centers. It belongs in the existing `ai_power_and_cooling` lane and closes a real discovery gap. The company is not buy-eligible: the July 17 close of USD 60.28 corresponds to a provider-reported USD 4.32 billion market cap, while the Q1 economic unit base plus GESA shares implies at least roughly USD 4.60 billion before awards and convertible dilution. Its expansion relies on USD 1.3 billion of senior notes, a closed USD 650 million revolver, other project obligations, substantial equity issuance, more than USD 1.26 billion of remaining 2026 capital spending, and concentrated customer execution.

SEI enters durable research-only coverage at `B`, not the active candidate set. Reachable public research is complete enough for an evidence-based buy-zero conclusion.

## Bottleneck Fit

- `what_could_become_scarce`: rapidly deployable generation, balance-of-plant equipment, commissioning, operations, and grid-independent capacity.
- `who_controls_or_removes_scarcity`: Solaris owns or contracts equipment and integrates deployment, controls, distribution, and operations.
- `who_can_monetize_into_shareholder_value`: the issuer must convert long contracts into free cash flow after financing, capex, VIE, and dilution claims.
- `public_security_expression`: NYSE-listed SEI directly expresses the exposure.
- `early_small_misunderstood_or_newly_public`: the oilfield-to-AI-power transition and Up-C/project structures can make the exposure underfollowed, but not automatically cheap.

## Evidence Gathered

- Q1 2026 revenue was USD 196.239 million; Power Solutions revenue was USD 128.538 million and deployed capacity reached about 910 MW.
- A February agreement covers more than 500 MW for ten years plus a five-year option, with a parent guarantee and a 50% remaining-rent termination payment.
- An April agreement covers more than 600 MW for another investment-grade technology-company affiliate over ten years plus a five-year option.
- The financing presentation describes more than 2,000 MW of contracted capacity and an expected operated fleet of about 3,100 MW by the end of 2029. These are meaningful scarcity-conversion signals, not proof of realized free cash flow.

## Analysis

- One customer represented 52% of Q1 revenue, and the largest power deployments remain concentrated among three AI-compute customers.
- Remaining 2026 capital spending was approximately USD 1.263 billion. Equipment orders, commissioning, gas supply, site readiness, and customer schedules create material execution risk.
- The May financing close included USD 1.3 billion of 6.375% senior notes due May 15, 2031 and a USD 650 million secured revolver. The offering presentation estimated 3.9 times annualized issuer-level leverage while excluding other obligations; covenants beginning September 30 and early material-contract termination can restrict availability or force repayment.
- The company states that it issued approximately USD 582 million of equity alongside debt. Variable-interest-entity and non-controlling-interest structures complicate how project economics accrue to common shareholders.
- The July 1 GESA acquisition added installation, commissioning, operations, maintenance, and repair capability for approximately USD 55 million of cash and repaid debt plus 2,880,682 Class A shares. It strengthens the turnkey model but adds roughly 4% share dilution versus the Q1 economic share base, integration, international, and customer-retention risk without target financial statements.
- The USD 875-925 million fully deployed adjusted EBITDA illustration is explicitly not guidance and assumes the Q1 margin structure persists; it cannot be capitalized as a forecast.

## Gate assessment

- Mission gate: pass for research. Fast off-grid power and balance-of-plant delivery address a real AI infrastructure bottleneck.
- Evidence gate: conditional pass for research, fail for active promotion. Contract economics, deployment milestones, free cash flow, and customer identities remain incomplete.
- Entry gate: fail. Current value, leverage, capital spending, and execution leave insufficient failure tolerance for the satellite objective.
- Survival gate: conditional. Long contracts and financing improve capacity, but debt, covenants, capex, and concentration can transmit a delivery or customer shock.
- Opportunity cost: cash, NBIS after a complete promotion review, and mature power peers provide better optionality or evidence; none is a current buy.
