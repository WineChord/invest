## Bottleneck Fit

The scarce capability is a funded, interconnected, utility-scale campus that can deliver high-density AI-compute power on time. TeraWulf directly controls campus development and power infrastructure rather than merely supplying a thematic proxy. The July 6 filing materially changes the prior generic crypto-to-AI rejection: a subsidiary signed a 20-year Anthropic lease for about 401 MW of critical IT load, with phased delivery expected from the second half of 2027 through early 2028. The company release describes about USD 19 billion of contracted revenue over the initial term. Nasdaq common stock WULF is policy-eligible, but shareholder value depends on the lease surviving construction, project financing, tenant support, debt service, dilution, and operating execution.

## Evidence Gathered

The March 31 filing reported USD 34.0 million of Q1 revenue, including USD 21.0 million from high-performance-computing leases and USD 13.0 million from digital-asset mining. It also reported a USD 427.7 million net loss, USD 17.6 million of operating cash use, USD 712.8 million of investing cash use, and USD 127.8 million of selling, general and administrative expense, including USD 101.4 million of stock-based compensation. Cash and equivalents were USD 2.630 billion and total restricted cash was USD 462.7 million.

Balance-sheet and dilution risk remain central. Primary-source review identifies about USD 5.725 billion of principal maturities including convertible debt, 449.5 million quarter-end shares, 495.5 million May 5 shares after later issuance, 80,879,054 warrants including 73.58 million Google warrants, and 47,463,851 unvested employee restricted stock units at quarter end. The convertible principal and initial conversion prices correspond to roughly 190.8 million underlying shares before capped-call effects. The Anthropic filing does not provide complete capex responsibility, escalation, termination, tenant-credit support, guarantee, return-on-capital, or project-financing terms. A separate July 6 transaction agreed to sell TeraWulf's 50.1% Abernathy joint-venture interest for about USD 530 million in staged payments.

The completed July 13 close was USD 20.89. That is a research snapshot, not an executable quote. Using the May 5 filing share count implies a basic equity value above USD 10 billion before warrants, employee awards, and convertible dilution. Headline contracted revenue therefore cannot be treated as current revenue, backlog margin, free cash flow, or per-share value.

## Risks and Kill Criteria

- Project financing or build cost makes the campus economics uneconomic for common equity.
- Delivery slips materially beyond early 2028 or tenant acceptance becomes disputed.
- Tenant support, termination, or capex terms leave TeraWulf with uncompensated construction risk.
- Debt, warrants, stock compensation, or equity issuance grow faster than credible per-share cash flow.
- Residual bitcoin economics or power-price volatility consume capital needed for contracted infrastructure.

## Decision

WULF receives research-only dashboard visibility at C+. It is not promoted and is not buy-eligible. Buy zero shares and sell zero shares. Reopen after lease-level economics, credible project financing, explicit support and termination protections, construction milestones, manageable dilution, and an attractive fully diluted valuation are available.
