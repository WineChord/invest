## Bottleneck Fit

Terrestrial Energy is developing the Integral Molten Salt Reactor, an advanced nuclear plant intended to convert licensing, fuel availability, manufacturing, project finance, and utility acceptance into deployable clean firm power. It is a direct advanced-reactor expression and has traded on Nasdaq under IMSR since October 29, 2025. The company is early and newly public enough to matter to the satellite mission, but regulators, fuel suppliers, customers, and first-of-a-kind project economics control the path from design intellectual property to shareholder cash flow.

- what_could_become_scarce: Licensed, fueled, financeable advanced-reactor capacity and credible first-project sites.
- who_controls_or_removes_scarcity: Terrestrial Energy controls the design and site-development work, while regulators, fuel suppliers, customers, financiers, and constructors control deployment dependencies.
- who_can_monetize_into_shareholder_value: Shareholders benefit only if licensing and site progress convert into funded plants and recurring economics faster than dilution.

## Evidence Gathered

At March 31, the company reported USD 76.946 million of cash and USD 198.018 million of short-term investments, plus USD 14.898 million of long-term investments. Convertible notes had converted, leaving no funded debt or interest accrual in Q1. The company nevertheless had no significant commercial revenue, a USD 11.931 million operating loss, USD 10.503 million net loss, USD 9.093 million of operating cash use, and USD 11.506 million of investing cash use.

The filing reported 82,242,434 common shares and 23,692,820 exchangeable economic interests represented through special voting shares at March 31. It also reported 30,276,119 warrants at a weighted exercise price of USD 8.24, 17,550,369 options, 1,232,794 restricted stock units, and a plan authorizing up to 38,741,269 shares. These layers make weighted-average or vendor market capitalization alone an incomplete measure of fully diluted value.

The completed July 13 close was USD 5.395. It is a research snapshot, not an executable quote. Applying the common plus exchangeable economic interests implies about USD 571 million of basic economic value before warrants, options, awards, and future project capital. Liquidity supports the current research runway, but it does not fund or validate a first commercial nuclear plant.

On June 18, Terrestrial Energy and the Texas A&M University System announced ground-lease and research-and-development agreements covering an exclusive 77-acre site at the RELLIS campus. The agreements permit site characterization and environmental work for a future Nuclear Regulatory Commission construction-permit application, and the company opened a local office. This materially improves site-control and project-formation evidence, but further A&M board approvals, an actual permit, funded construction, fuel, customers, and project economics remain unresolved.

## Risks and Kill Criteria

- Licensing milestones slip without a credible corrective path.
- Fuel availability or qualification cannot support the planned reactor timeline.
- First-project capital or customer economics require repeated common-equity financing.
- Fully diluted share growth outruns validated technical and commercial milestones.
- A competing reactor or conventional power solution reaches financeable deployment first.

## Decision

IMSR receives research-only dashboard visibility at C+. It is not promoted and is not buy-eligible. Buy zero shares and sell zero shares. Reopen after concrete licensing progress, a credible fuel and supply-chain plan, a funded customer project, transparent first-project economics, controlled dilution, and an attractive fully diluted valuation.
