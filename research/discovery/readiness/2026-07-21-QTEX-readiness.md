## Conclusion

QTEX provides a small, awkward public expression of cryogenic quantum interconnect and additive-electronics manufacturing that conventional screens could miss. It merits research-only `C+` coverage, not active promotion or a purchase. The completed July 20 close was USD 1.18, and the basic market value was approximately USD 58 million before substantial options, warrants, and pre-funded-warrant exposure.

## Bottleneck Fit

- `what_could_become_scarce`: qualified cryogenic interconnect, control and readout connectivity, and manufacturable high-density quantum packaging.
- `who_controls_or_removes_scarcity`: QTEX claims a monolithic cryogenic-interconnect architecture and owns acquired AME and Fabrica manufacturing assets.
- `who_can_monetize_into_shareholder_value`: only a platform that converts qualification into repeat orders, positive margins, controlled burn, and low dilution can retain the economics.
- `public_security_expression`: Nasdaq-listed QTEX is directly tradable under the current policy.
- `early_small_misunderstood_or_newly_public`: the legacy pivot, tiny scale, ticker change, and acquired-asset structure make the exposure easy to miss and hard to value.

## Evidence Gathered

The acquired AME and Fabrica business reported approximately USD 5.52 million of 2025 revenue but an approximately USD 17.35 million operating shortfall, following USD 6.04 million revenue and an approximately USD 25.69 million shortfall in 2024. The legacy issuer pivot, going-concern language, an April acquisition with contingent consideration, a May USD 10 million financing, approximately 49.15 million June 1 base shares, 2.77 million pre-funded warrants, and layered warrants leave shareholder capture and survival unclear. June and July disclosed orders improve technical relevance but omit material price, customer qualification, margin, repeatability, and production evidence.

## Gate assessment

- Mission gate: pass for research; cryogenic connectivity can become a quantum-compute bottleneck.
- Evidence gate: fail for promotion; customer qualification and durable product economics are unproved.
- Entry gate: fail; nominal price and small capitalization do not compensate for losses and dilution.
- Survival gate: fail for investment; cash burn, financing cadence, contingent consideration, and going-concern evidence are material.
- Opportunity cost: IONQ, XNDU, IQMX, INFQ, and cash offer cleaner or more complete comparison points even though none is a current buy.

Reachable public research is complete enough for buy zero. Reopen after independently verified production qualification, quantified repeat orders, positive product gross margin, audited post-acquisition revenue, lower cash burn, and a fully reconciled diluted capitalization.
