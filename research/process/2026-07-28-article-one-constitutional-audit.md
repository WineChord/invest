# Article 1 Constitutional Audit

Review date: 2026-07-28

Policy transition: `v1.1` to `v1.2`

Scope: capital deployment, no-action decisions, discovery recall, readiness, position construction, validation, and publication safety

## Review Method

The audit combined three independent read-only reviews:

- capital deployment and valuation mathematics;
- discovery, freshness, and false-negative bias;
- avoidable-ruin controls versus inactivity bias.

The reviews disagreed only on implementation emphasis. All preserved broker truth, current primary evidence, survival, allowed-asset, human-control, and publication boundaries. All concluded that zero exposure needs stronger counterfactual proof and that unrelated repository completeness must not become an allocation veto.

## Conclusion

The operating system was partially misaligned with Article 1. Its truth, broker-confirmation, freshness, survival, audit, allowed-asset, human-control, and publication safeguards protect the mission and remain binding. The misalignment was in the objective function below those safeguards: the repository could prove completeness and justify inactivity more easily than it could prove that zero exposure beat the smallest mission-consistent staged exposure.

This audit does not retroactively prove that a recent no-action decision was wrong and does not create an order. It changes the burden of proof for future decisions.

## Evidence

- The 2026-07-28 completed-close research snapshot recorded USD 5,761.58 confirmed cash, USD 7,539.91 research NAV, and 76.4144% liquidity-option weight. The latest confirmed return-seeking deployment was on 2026-07-08.
- Fifteen indexed `completed_no_action` runs occurred after that deployment. Daily cadence overstates independent decision count, so the number is an accountability signal rather than proof that any one decision was incorrect.
- The latest broad scan covered 7,649 usable issuers by identity, but issuer-profile semantic coverage was 0%. It returned 17 organic candidates while 42 known proxies were recalled only through hard-coded ticker support. Known-proxy recall is a regression check, not evidence that unknown business models were found.
- The current watchlist review covered every non-removed symbol, but most rows were unchanged. Coverage is useful for continuity; requiring equal research depth for every row would tax discovery without improving allocation.
- Current RKLB and ASTS weights are already capable of affecting portfolio outcomes. The larger Article 1 risk is future contribution dilution and the absence of a durable path from initial exposure to a mission-relevant or exited position.

## Rules Preserved

- No automatic trading and no inferred broker facts.
- Broker-confirmed, append-only account records.
- Current primary evidence, source dates, validity windows, and material filing review.
- Survival, capital structure, dilution, permanent impairment, customer concentration, governance, and tradability checks.
- No leverage, margin, options, short selling, crypto tokens, private-share orders, OTC orders, or non-U.S.-listed account actions under the current policy.
- Public-release embargo, sensitive-field controls, anti-signal boundaries, and historical auditability.

These rules are not secondary comforts. They prevent avoidable ruin and therefore serve Article 1.

## Conflicts Corrected

### Zero exposure lacked an equal burden of proof

Future no-action allocation decisions must identify the strongest counterfactual, compare zero with the smallest mission-consistent staged exposure, classify the blocker, explain why size cannot bound it, state cash opportunity cost, and set a next evidence deadline.

Volatility, concentration, early-stage status, non-profitability, or bounded uncertainty are not standalone vetoes. Zero remains valid when even the smallest feasible trade fails mission impact, evidence, entry, survival, permanent-impairment, or opportunity-cost tests.

### Cash and reserve could become hidden objectives

Mission accountability now uses `liquidity_option_weight`: confirmed cash plus confirmed liquidity-reserve market value divided by research NAV. A symbolic purchase cannot reset the clock. The 60% weight and 45-day, 90-day, and 180-day periods trigger progressively deeper Article 1 reviews, never automatic orders.

### Repository completeness could veto an unrelated opportunity

Target readiness and opportunity-set sufficiency now control allocation. Repository health remains mandatory operational work, but bounded discovery debt, dashboard work, formatting debt, and unrelated low-priority research cannot become portfolio-wide vetoes.

### Discovery rewarded closure more than recall

The R0-R3 funnel separates a cheap lead, researchable evidence, fair comparability, and promotion readiness. R1 and R2 candidates may remain open under dated service levels. Only R3 carries the full filing, valuation, independent-review, and dashboard burden. This makes honest incubation cheaper than premature rejection.

Search quality now distinguishes identity coverage, business-semantic coverage, organic recall, known-proxy-only recall, exploratory sampling, and time to first researchable review. A hard-coded known proxy cannot prove unknown-company discovery.

### Position construction stopped at “starter”

Future contenders need initial and fully underwritten weight ranges, permanent-impairment budgets, downside/base/upside/exceptional portfolio effects, contribution-dilution sensitivity, scale or hold milestones, exit milestones, and a stage-review date.

A starter is bounded-uncertainty optionality, not evidence acquisition. Holding a stock does not create issuer evidence and can create endowment bias. An initial position without a credible path to mission-relevant scale should not be opened merely to create activity.

## Review Parameters

The following are adjustable review parameters, not constitutional constants:

- high liquidity-option weight: 60%;
- mission-pressure review: 45 days;
- opportunity-set reset: 90 days;
- strategy-feasibility review: 180 days.

The parameters should be revised if they create calendar-driven trading, symbolic trades, false precision, or excessive review cost without improving mission outcomes.

## Regression Expectations

- Reject a no-action conclusion based only on volatility, concentration, youth, non-profitability, generic uncertainty, or a non-conjunctive price statement.
- Reject a no-action allocation with deployable liquidity when the strongest counterfactual, zero-versus-smallest-position comparison, blocker classification, waiting cost, and next evidence deadline are missing.
- Reject a current buy or add when portfolio-impact, permanent-impairment, stage, and path-to-scale fields are missing.
- Keep target-critical filing, survival, capital-structure, and freshness gaps blocking.
- Allow bounded R1 and R2 discovery debt without making the entire allocation unready.
- Do not treat zero business-semantic coverage or hard-coded proxy recall as complete broad discovery.
- Keep exact unexpired share counts, target weights, and live scale ladders private until the publication embargo expires.

## Persistent Enforcement

Every powered-on repository interaction now starts with a proportional Article 1 preflight and ends with an Article 1 postflight. The preflight identifies the mission contribution or the necessary truth, survival, human-control, audit, clone-portability, or public-safety boundary being preserved. The postflight checks that the result improved the probability of finding, funding, sizing, or holding rare qualifying outcomes instead of adding ceremony or inactivity.

The constitution carries the supremacy and lower-level-yield contract. The proportional preflight and postflight are repeated only at active routing surfaces: the agent router, repository skill, system specification, current policy, and core operating templates. Deterministic validation checks these canonical surfaces for their applicable supremacy, preflight, postflight, and lower-level-yield anchors. Removing an anchor requires an intentional validator and test change rather than allowing silent drift.

This enforcement remains proportional. It does not turn narrow maintenance into a full decision cycle, and a passing repository check does not make any security buy-eligible.

Apply policy `v1.2` to the next allocation decision and evaluate whether it changes:
