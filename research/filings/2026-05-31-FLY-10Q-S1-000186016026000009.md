## Review Summary

Firefly is a direct space infrastructure candidate, not a generic thematic proxy. SEC submissions confirm FLY as a Nasdaq-listed common stock, and the May 2026 filing cluster creates enough public evidence to move it from raw discovery into a research-only dashboard surface.

The current evidence does not support a buy or watchlist promotion. The Q1 2026 filing and companyfacts extract show Q1 revenue of about USD 80.9 million, Q1 net loss of about USD 96.7 million, cash and equivalents of about USD 326.2 million, debt of about USD 136.1 million, and roughly 160.2 million shares outstanding. At the 2026-05-29 close of USD 46.49, the refreshed market data implies market capitalization near USD 7.45 billion and price-to-sales near 40.7x trailing revenue. That setup is demanding for a company with material losses, cash-burn risk, and a short public trading record.

The offering and selling-holder filings matter. The S-1, 424B3, EFFECT, and Form 144 sequence means future allocation work must keep dilution, lockup, float, and insider or holder sale pressure in view. Those risks do not make the stock ineligible under policy v1.1, but they do prevent treating the candidate as ready for capital allocation today.

## Bottleneck and Peer Context

FLY belongs in the same broad space infrastructure opportunity set as RKLB, ASTS, LUNR, RDW, and KTOS. The evidence is still weaker than RKLB or ASTS for this account's first allocation because the current repository record for FLY has less public operating history, less margin-quality evidence, and more immediate offering and dilution uncertainty.

## Decision Use

Do not treat this review as a buy order. FLY is now research-only and visible in the dashboard-facing universe with market data, technical data, company metrics, valuation state, and analysis history. It remains incubating in discovery because the evidence-based conclusion is no-buy: current valuation, losses, runway and dilution risk, and short public record do not justify displacing RKLB, ASTS, or cash for the 2026-05-31 decision.

Promotion would require a materially better evidence and entry setup: cleaner post-offering float, improved cash runway or financing terms, contract and backlog quality that converts into gross margin, launch and spacecraft execution evidence, and a price that leaves room for asymmetric compounding.
