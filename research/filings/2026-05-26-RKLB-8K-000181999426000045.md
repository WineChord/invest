## Executive Conclusion

The filing weakens the near-term entry setup but does not break the long-term thesis. Rocket Lab created the ability to sell up to $3.0 billion of common stock through at-the-market and forward-sale structures. This is material dilution capacity, but at the current elevated share price it can also fund organic growth, acquisitions, and balance-sheet strength with less dilution per dollar than would have been possible at lower prices.

## Thesis Delta

The filing confirms that management may use the current valuation window to raise substantial capital. This is rational if proceeds fund high-return growth or strategic acquisitions, but it is negative if capital is deployed into low-return roll-up activity or if issuance becomes persistent share overhang.

## Valuation Delta

The valuation state moves from attractive operational evidence to fair-to-expensive entry. A first position can still be justified for the satellite account, but only with price discipline and staged sizing. Chasing a large gap after this filing would create an unfavorable risk/reward setup.

## Risk Delta

The principal risks are dilution, forward-sale complexity, acquisition discipline, and a stock-price reversal if market enthusiasm fades. The filing does not require selling because there are no existing confirmed shares, but it argues against using the entire first deposit on RKLB at an uncapped market price.
