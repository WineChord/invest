## Review

SpaceX is now a policy-eligible public-security candidate in form: Class A common stock under SPCX on Nasdaq and Nasdaq Texas. The offering priced 555,555,555 primary Class A shares at USD 135.00 per share, with a June 12, 2026 trade date and June 15, 2026 closing date.

The company is a direct cross-lane bottleneck candidate: launch, spacecraft manufacturing, Starlink connectivity, direct-to-device, AI compute infrastructure, and frontier AI platform exposure all map to existing repository lanes. This does not require a new lane, but it changes the opportunity-cost set for RKLB, ASTS, and AI compute names.

The entry gate does not pass on first review. First-day trading around USD 160.95 implies a roughly USD 2.1 trillion equity value using the post-offering Class A and Class B share count disclosed in the prospectus. That scale sharply reduces the probability of the tens-to-hundreds-times public-market outcome that this satellite account seeks. The filing also introduces controlled-company governance, Elon Musk voting control, xAI/X recast losses, stock-funded transaction risk, large future equity-plan capacity, and underwriter option or lockup dynamics that need a dedicated post-IPO review.

## Conclusion

SPCX should move from future-watch/not-tradable status into current public watchlist coverage, but it is not buy-ready and should not enter buy-zone consideration until a dedicated promotion review, valuation model, lockup/float review, and at least one public reporting update or thesis-preserving price dislocation.
