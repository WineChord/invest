The starting cash and offering receipts are a historical bridge input, not a claim that cash at October 2 equals that total. The cumulative operating and spending lines cover the intervening period. Tiny remaining pre-funded exercise proceeds and the disclosed USD 2,000 note conversion are immaterial at the table's precision.

Downside assumes transition problems and legacy-supply disruption destroy most expansion value. Its residual recovery is not a floor: complete equity impairment remains possible. Downside growth spending is a net amount after any project receipts, so a second DOE receipt would be incorrect.

Base normalized FCFF can be decomposed illustratively into approximately USD 40 million from HALEU, USD 70 million from domestic commercial enrichment and USD 50 million from legacy/other operations. The domestic contribution assumes a successfully deployed business; it is not present earnings. The USD 2.7 billion gross growth-spending assumption represents a multiyear industrial program. Neither that total nor its adequate funding is disclosed guidance. Ordinary spending variance and issuance price must therefore be stressed rather than presented as resolved facts.

Upside requires substantially more capacity and adoption than the proposed initial HALEU scale, strong margins, and a growth franchise worth 22 times normalized FCFF. Exceptional requires an even larger durable business and further reinvestment opportunities. These cases cannot be reached merely by completing the existing award. Terminal multiples are explicit analyst growth/capitalization assumptions, not inherited peer valuations.

For each case, terminal net cash equals starting cash plus offering proceeds, cumulative operating cash, separately credited project receipts and financing proceeds, less growth spending and cash principal repayments. Conversion-premium shares are solved jointly with price:

```text
premium_shares_millions = sum(max(0, principal_millions / strike - principal_millions / terminal_price))
terminal_price = residual_common_equity_millions / (economic_basic_shares_millions + financing_shares + compensation_shares + cash_warrant_shares + premium_shares)
present_value = terminal_price / (1.13 ^ 7)
```

Terminal price is a simplifying proxy for conversion settlement prices. Actual timing and adjustment provisions can change dilution. Upside and exceptional assume the common warrants exercise for cash before their respective expirations; both USD 2 billion of proceeds and the full corresponding shares are included. Their terminal values exceed every strike, but terminal price alone does not prove timely exercise. Later appreciation, cashless exercise or earlier settlement requires a different funding/share bridge. No case counts full note-conversion shares while also deducting all note principal.

The August 10 interim statements [G1] explicitly give 77,029,657 issued and outstanding shares at June 30. The August proxy [G2] repeats that count as of July 31. Quarterly basic and diluted weighted-average EPS denominators are different concepts and must not replace actual ownership. The authorization of additional share capacity is likewise not evidence of issuance.

The completed USD 100 million note financing [G3] is convertible initially at USD 16, implying 6.25 million shares. Notes carry 3.75% interest with a possible 1.25-point increase and mature in 2031. A conversion scenario adds the appropriate shares and removes the converted debt; it must not charge both claims in full.

Using actual shares and the reference price gives USD 757.97 million basic equity. June cash and short-term deposits of USD 159.155 million, approximately USD 147.5 million remaining base acquisition consideration after the advance, the USD 100 million financing and approximately USD 102 million total debt imply roughly USD 9.7 million residual net cash before fees, purchase adjustments and intervening cash movements [G3, G4, G5]. On that explicitly provisional bridge, post-transaction EV is approximately **USD 748 million**, or **9.35 times** management's approximately USD 80 million combined adjusted EBITDA framework. An automatic EV based on the old denominator and pre-transaction cash overstates apparent cheapness.

A sensitivity of USD 35–50 million normalized annual equity cash generation at 16–20 times capitalization corresponds to approximately USD 7–12 per share before additional future dilution. This is an analyst cash-return sensitivity, not guidance or a precise fair-value range. Its cash input requires conversion of adjusted EBITDA after taxes, maintenance investment, working capital, financing and compensation economics. At the current research reference, exceptional future outcomes remain possible, but a larger commitment is not established by the EBITDA multiple alone. The expired earlier private entry ceiling is not a permanent valuation rule.
