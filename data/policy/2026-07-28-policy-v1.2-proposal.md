# Policy Change Proposal

Proposal ID: `policy-v1.2-article-one-mission-accountability`

Created at: 2026-07-28

Current policy version: `v1.1`

Proposed policy version: `v1.2`

## Problem

Policy `v1.1` correctly preserves cash optionality, but it does not define when prolonged high cash, repeated no-action decisions, globally coupled research-readiness requirements, or permanently immaterial starter positions stop serving the supreme mission.

The repository can therefore satisfy freshness, coverage, validation, and risk controls while still drifting toward inactivity. That failure mode protects against false positives but can also reduce the probability that a rare extreme winner receives capital early enough and at a weight large enough to affect the account.

## Evidence

- [CONSTITUTION.md](../../CONSTITUTION.md) makes multi-decade asymmetric compounding the highest-order constraint and allows volatility, concentration, uncertainty, and long time horizons when they do not create avoidable ruin.
- The operating-run index through 2026-07-28 contains 26 decision runs, including 16 explicit no-action runs and four runs linked to confirmed buy executions.
- The 2026-07-28 completed-close research snapshot records USD 5,761.58 confirmed cash, USD 0 confirmed liquidity reserve, USD 7,539.91 research NAV, and a 76.4144% liquidity-option weight.
- The current buy-zone surface contains no executable entry limit. That can be correct for a single cycle, but the process needs a deterministic review trigger if the state persists.
- The current global readiness model can let unrelated candidate or dashboard completeness block allocation even when the decision-critical evidence for a superior target and its closest opportunity-cost set is current.

## Current Rule

Policy `v1.1` allows monthly contributions to remain unspent and permits cash or an approved short-duration U.S. Treasury reserve when no candidate passes mission, evidence, and entry gates. It does not:

- classify uncertainty by whether it should block, reduce size, or remain process debt;
- require portfolio-impact math or a path from starter position to meaningful position;
- distinguish target-level allocation readiness from whole-repository health;
- trigger a constitutional review after prolonged high cash or inactivity;
- place a burden of proof on repeated no-action decisions.

## Proposed Rule

Adopt policy `v1.2` with the following Article 1 controls:

1. Treat the supreme mission as the controlling policy test. Truth, broker confirmation, source freshness, no automatic trading, auditability, publication safety, and avoidable-ruin controls remain binding because they protect the mission.
2. Classify uncertainty as decision-critical, sizing, or process uncertainty. Only decision-critical uncertainty automatically blocks a target. Sizing uncertainty must be tested through smaller or staged exposure, but zero remains valid when the smallest feasible trade fails mission impact or opportunity cost. Unrelated repository-health debt cannot veto a decision-ready target.
3. Require every active allocation contender to show dilution-adjusted extreme-upside feasibility, portfolio impact at a feasible initial and scaled weight, and a source-backed path to scale or exit.
4. Keep monthly stock deployment optional, but treat a liquidity-option weight of at least 60% plus 45, 90, and 180 days without a mission-relevant deployment as escalating mission-review triggers. Report the latest confirmed return-seeking buy separately; a symbolic trade does not reset mission accountability.
5. Require repeated no-action decisions to identify concrete decision-critical blockers, the best counterfactual candidate, conjunctive evidence-and-price conditions, and the next review date. “Research incomplete” is not sufficient when evidence is publicly reachable.
6. Preserve staged entries, but do not let a starter remain permanently immaterial. Each starter needs explicit evidence milestones for scaling, holding, or exiting. A starter is bounded-uncertainty optionality, not evidence acquisition, and owning it must not substitute for issuer evidence.
7. Let target-level readiness and opportunity-set sufficiency control allocation. Whole-repository quality remains mandatory operational work, but unrelated formatting, dashboard, or low-priority research debt is not investment evidence.
8. Keep current account-action exclusions. Non-U.S.-listed and private companies may still be researched as bottleneck-map intelligence and public-peer comparators without becoming account orders.

## Expected Benefit

- Raises the probability that rare qualifying opportunities receive capital before all uncertainty disappears and the market reprices the evidence.
- Makes cash an accountable temporary option rather than a hidden objective.
- Connects company upside to feasible position size and portfolio consequence.
- Keeps discovery broad while preventing low-priority process completeness from vetoing a decision-ready target.
- Preserves truth, survival, auditability, human execution control, and publication safety.

## New Risks

- Mission-pressure reviews could be misread as instructions to trade merely because time passed.
- Portfolio-impact analysis can create false precision if scenario assumptions are not source-backed.
- Staged position construction can add complexity and encourage overtrading if evidence milestones are vague.
- Decoupling repository health from target readiness could allow research debt to accumulate unless it remains visible and time-bounded.

The adopted policy addresses these risks by making all time thresholds review triggers rather than orders, requiring scenario ranges instead of point forecasts, keeping broker execution manual, and retaining repository-health validation separately from target investment readiness.

## Rollback Plan

Revert future decisions to policy `v1.1` if the new controls create calendar-driven trading, weaken source or survival standards, generate false-precision sizing, or allow material opportunity-cost evidence to be ignored. Historical decisions remain governed by the policy version cited when they were made.
