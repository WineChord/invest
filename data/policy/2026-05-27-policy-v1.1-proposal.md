# Policy Change Proposal

Proposal ID: `policy-v1.1-cash-management-reserve`

Created at: 2026-05-27

Current policy version: `v1.0`

Proposed policy version: `v1.1`

## Problem

The account's mission is multi-decade asymmetric compounding, not forced monthly deployment. The current policy allows holding cash, but it does not explicitly allow a short-duration Treasury ETF such as SGOV as a cash-management reserve. Without a clear rule, future monthly decisions may incorrectly feel pressure to deploy every USD 888 contribution into common stocks or may treat a cash-equivalent ETF as part of the return-seeking satellite allocation.

## Evidence

- The user approved a durable rule that monthly contributions do not need to be fully deployed and that idle liquidity may be parked in SGOV while waiting for rare high-conviction opportunities.
- iShares states that SGOV seeks to track an index composed of 0-3 month U.S. Treasury securities and describes it as a tool for capital preservation and liquidity management.
- Schwab International materials state that Schwab One International accounts can trade U.S. stocks and ETFs, subject to account eligibility and local restrictions.
- Schwab explains that equity and ETF trades settle T+1 and that cash-account clients may trade with unsettled cash, but selling the newly purchased security before the original sale settles can create a good faith violation.

## Current Rule

Policy `v1.0` allows U.S.-listed common stocks and ADRs with public disclosures and normal retail liquidity. It excludes broad funds that duplicate the user's core Nasdaq technology exposure. It does not explicitly allow SGOV or equivalent short-duration Treasury ETFs.

## Proposed Rule

Allow SGOV or a materially equivalent short-duration U.S. Treasury ETF or Treasury money-market vehicle only as a liquidity reserve and cash-management instrument. This reserve is not a return-seeking satellite allocation, not a benchmark, and not evidence that cash must always be invested.

Monthly decisions may choose `no trade`, `hold cash`, or `park idle cash in SGOV` when no eligible asymmetric opportunity passes the mission, evidence, and entry gates.

When a target common stock becomes attractive, the account may sell SGOV first and then buy the target stock after the broker shows sufficient funds available to trade. For Schwab International cash accounts, if unsettled SGOV sale proceeds are used to buy a stock, the newly purchased stock should not be sold before the SGOV sale settles unless the broker confirms that doing so will not create a settlement violation.

## Expected Benefit

- Preserves optionality for rare high-upside opportunities.
- Reduces pressure to buy merely because cash arrived.
- Keeps idle liquidity aligned with avoidable-ruin discipline.
- Makes cash-management trades auditable instead of hiding them inside "cash" assumptions.

## New Risks

- SGOV and equivalent ETFs are not cash. They have market price movement, distributions, bid/ask spreads, fund expenses, possible tracking error, tax effects, settlement timing, and broker-specific restrictions.
- If SGOV becomes too large or too sticky, the account could drift from asymmetric compounding into cash drag.
- If the user sells SGOV and immediately buys a stock in a cash account, later selling that stock before the SGOV sale settles can create a good faith violation.

## Rollback Plan

Revert to `v1.0` if SGOV or equivalent reserve instruments create operational complexity, unexpected account restrictions, tax burden, or mission drift. Existing SGOV positions would then be treated as liquidity to be sold when practical, subject to broker confirmation and settlement rules.
